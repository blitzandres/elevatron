# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 What Elevatron is

Elevatron is a **named thought-experiment**: a magnetically-pinched central beam, wrapped by an external ring/solenoid magnet, conditioned by lasers, conjectured to act at once as a **conductor** (carrying current/power along its length) and as a **structure** (a self-reforming "pillar" that contributes rigidity toward space access).

The idea was articulated conversationally — *"the beam in the middle of the circle magnet will serve as a structure and conductor."* That intuition is physically literate: a current-carrying plasma column really does generate confining magnetic tension and really is an excellent conductor. The job of this repository is to take the intuition seriously and follow it until the physics either supports it or stops it.

## 1.2 What Elevatron is not

- It is **not** a claim that a working launcher can be built.
- It is **not** a classical space elevator (a material tether in tension from a counterweight; see Edwards 2003).
- It is **not** a weapons or high-energy-device build guide. The experimental chapter is restricted to safe, educational demonstrations.

## 1.3 Scope and method

We proceed in the order a thesis committee would expect:

1. **Establish the physics** of the magnetic pinch from first principles (§2).
2. **State the dual-role conjecture precisely** and test each half with order-of-magnitude calculations (§3).
3. **Place the laser** correctly in the architecture, distinguishing the roles it can and cannot play (§4).
4. **Sketch a reference architecture** so the concept is concrete enough to criticize (§5).
5. **Run the feasibility numbers** — energy, stability, atmosphere — and deliver a verdict (§6).
6. **Demonstrate the real physics at the bench** with safe apparatus (§7).
7. **Conclude** with what survives and what the credible near-term cousins are (§8).

## 1.4 The three parent fields

| Field | Key result we lean on | Anchor citation |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Magnetic pinch | A beam carrying current *I* self-confines above the Bennett threshold | Bennett (1934) |
| Magnetized inertial fusion | Lasers + magnetic compression genuinely combine (MagLIF) | Slutz et al. (2010); Gomez et al. (2014) |
| Beamed-energy propulsion | Ground/space lasers can deliver useful thrust to a vehicle | Kantrowitz (1972); Myrabo & Lewis (lightcraft) |
| Self-focusing relativistic beams | A pinched beam can stay collimated over long ranges | Bennett (1934); Greason (q-drive / beam-pull concept) |

Every one of these is real and published. Elevatron asks whether *combining* them yields a structure — and §6 is where that question gets its answer.
