# Annotated Citations

A reading path through the literature, grouped by the role each source plays in the Elevatron evaluation. Full entries in [`bibliography.bib`](bibliography.bib).

## The pinch itself
- **Bennett (1934), *Phys. Rev.* 45, 890** — the foundational paper. Introduces the self-focusing of current-carrying streams and the relation $\mu_0 I^2/8\pi = N k_B(T_e+T_i)$. Everything in §2 descends from here.
- **Krall & Trivelpiece, *Principles of Plasma Physics*** — textbook treatment of pinch equilibria and MHD stability.
- **Chen, *Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion*** — clear derivation of Alfvén speed and the sausage/kink modes that set the µs instability clock (§2.4, §6.2).

## Laser + magnetic pinch (the real combination)
- **Slutz et al. (2010), *Phys. Plasmas* 17, 056303** — the MagLIF concept: laser preheat + axial magnetization + Z-pinch compression. The closest existing system to "laser + pinch."
- **Gomez et al. (2014), *PRL* 113, 155003** — first fusion-relevant MagLIF experimental results. Proof the combination operates.

## Beamed-energy propulsion (the surviving launch role)
- **Kantrowitz (1972)** — the founding proposal for ground-based laser launch.
- **Myrabo (2003), AIAA 2003-4842** — flight demonstration of beam-riding lightcraft.
- **Forward (1984), *J. Spacecraft Rockets* 21, 187** — laser-pushed light sails; momentum transfer by photons.
- **Lubin (2016), *JBIS* 69, 40** — directed-energy roadmap; the GW-class scale used in the §6 energy budget.

## Channel guiding through atmosphere
- **Kasparian & Wolf (2008), *Opt. Express* 16, 466** — physics of laser filamentation in air.
- **Houard et al. (2023), *Nature Photonics* 17, 231** — field demonstration of guiding a real discharge along a laser filament. Anchors the §4.1 pre-ionization role.

## Self-focusing beams for power transfer
- **Greason — relativistic-beam power architecture / q-drive** — uses self-pinch to keep a beam collimated over long range; the conductor/momentum-conduit role of §3.1. (Conference/whitepaper material; see Centauri Dreams coverage for an accessible summary.)

## Reference point for the classical alternative
- **Edwards & Westling (2003), NIAC** — the material-tether space elevator, included to contrast with what Elevatron is *not* (§1.2).

## Concept provenance

The Elevatron conjecture — *a central pinched beam serving simultaneously as conductor and structure inside a ring magnet, conditioned by lasers* — was articulated by the author (A. Blitz) in an exploratory human–AI dialogue on **2026-06-12**, and motivated by popular coverage of relativistic-beam space propulsion (nextbigfuture.com) and laser-launch concepts (toughsf.blogspot.com). This repository is the rigorous, cited follow-through to that conversation: it neither inflates the idea nor dismisses it, but tests it against first-principles physics and ties each conclusion to the sources above.

> Citation style note: this project deliberately treats *citation as the experiment* — the test of a speculative idea is whether, claim by claim, it can be attached to a real source and survive the attachment. See `docs/08-conclusions.md` §8.4.
